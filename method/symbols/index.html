Kernels from Fourier symbols

A standard kernel plus a smooth remainder

Many kernels of interest are known by their Fourier transform rather than in closed form: the potential of a layer of finite depth, a screened kernel, the response of a membrane embedded in a fluid. For a radial kernel with symbol $\hat s(k)$, $k = |\mathbf k|$ (or a tensor kernel with isotropic mobilities, $\hat G = a(k)(I - \hat k\hat k^T) + b(k)\hat k\hat k^T$), SymbolKernel writes the symbol as that of a standard kernel base with the same large-$k$ behavior plus a remainder,

\[\hat s(k) = \hat s_{\text{base}}(k) + \Delta(k).\]

$\Delta$ decays in $k$, so its kernel $R$ is smooth in real space, and is tabulated once by Hankel transforms ($x = k\rho$):

Output
:value$R(\rho) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^\infty \Delta\,J_0(x)\,k\,dk$
:gradient$R'(\rho)/\rho = -\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^\infty \Delta\,k^2\,\frac{J_1(x)}{x}\,k\,dk$ ($\nabla R = (R'/\rho)\,r$)
:potential_gradient$P'(\rho)/\rho = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^\infty \Delta\,\frac{J_1(x)}{x}\,k\,dk$ ($\Delta P = R$)
:potential$P(\rho) - P(0) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^\infty \Delta\,\frac{1 - J_0(x)}{k^2}\,k\,dk$
tensor$R = \alpha I + \beta rr^T$, $\alpha = A_0 + A_2$, $\beta = -2A_2/\rho^2$, $A_0 = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int s\,J_0\,k\,dk$, $A_2 = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int d\,J_2\,k\,dk$, $s = (\Delta a + \Delta b)/2$, $d = (\Delta b - \Delta a)/2$

The $k$ integrals are Gauss–Legendre on panels (uniform on $[0, k_{\max}]$, or on given breakpoints when $\Delta$ has structure near $k = 0$); the table is piecewise Chebyshev on panels of width about scale up to $\rho_c$ (RadialTable), with a closed-form far field beyond it if one is given.

The numerics keep the standard kernel for everything singular (the local corrections, Ewald's short range, the self terms, preconditioners) and add only the smooth remainder: to Ewald's long-range multiplier (Stokeslet3D; BiharmonicGradient2D with a far field, whose whole kernel is sampled there), or as a local sum over the pairs closer than $\rho_c$ where the whole kernel decays (LaplaceKernel3D, BiharmonicGradient2D without a far field), or through SmoothConvolution. The local sum is all-pairs when $\rho_c$ exceeds the film (a depth comparable to its size): give the remainder a far field, and use the SymbolKernel itself as the kernel for LaplaceKernel3D, to sum it by Ewald instead.

H = 0.3     # the 3D Laplace kernel over a layer of depth H, the value fixed on its floor: tanh(kH)/(2k)
K = SymbolKernel(k -> tanh(k * H) / (2k); base = LaplaceKernel3D(), scale = H, outputs = (:value,),
                 remainder = k -> -1 / (k * (exp(2k * H) + 1)))
K(Vec2(0.1, 0.2))             # the whole kernel, 1/(4πρ) + R(ρ)
kernel_remainder(K, 0.5)      # R alone
E = Evaluator(LaplaceKernel3D(), ad; remainder = K)

Conditions on the base and the remainder

  • The base must match the symbol at large $k$ (the same singularity, so that $\Delta$ decays and $R$ is smooth) and at small $k$ ($k\,\Delta$ integrable at $k = 0$, so the Hankel integrals converge; for a gradient output only $k^3\Delta$). It is a standard kernel (Stokeslet3D, LaplaceKernel3D) or one with a kernel_symbol method. It is given, not extracted from the symbol. The constructor checks the small-$k$ limit and throws when the integrals diverge.
  • remainder gives $\Delta$ in a numerically stable form when the plain difference cancels (as for the finite depth above, whose difference is $-1/(k(e^{2kH} + 1))$).
  • A remainder not integrable at $k = 0$ ($\Delta \sim k^{-4}$) has a gradient only up to a multiple of $r$: regularize = true subtracts the $x \to 0$ value of $J_1(x)/x$ (:gradient output only), and the base must fix that multiple.
  • How the remainder decays. A remainder that decays exponentially in $k$ (a finite depth) is resolved by the default $k_{\max} = 25/$scale. One that decays algebraically (a screened kernel $e^{-\kappa\rho}/4\pi\rho$, $\Delta \sim -\kappa^2/(4k^3)$) has a cone or a logarithm at $\rho = 0$: the constructor classifies the tail (K.decay === :algebraic), extends $k_{\max}$ (up to 64 times) until the tail is below tol of $|R(0)|$, and warns when it cannot. An Evaluator then takes the near corrections of the whole kernel, base plus remainder. As a remainder the Ewald sums refuse it; the SymbolKernel used as the kernel itself is summed by Ewald, its remainder split by weight between the long and the short range (Fast sums). A tensor one is tabulated as $(\alpha, A_2/\rho)$, since $\beta = -2A_2/\rho^2$ then grows like $1/\rho$ at $\rho = 0$.

cached_kernel(build, key) keeps a table built once per key (a depth, a screening length) for the session.
