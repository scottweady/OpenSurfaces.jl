Geometry

Conventions

$\Omega$ lies in the plane $z = 0$; points are Vec2. Its boundary $\Gamma$ is oriented with $\Omega$ on the left: outer curves counterclockwise, holes clockwise. $n$ is the outward unit normal (out of $\Omega$, so into a hole), $\tau$ the unit tangent, $n = (\tau_2, -\tau_1)$, and the curvature $\kappa > 0$ where $\Omega$ is locally convex.

Boundary panels

discretize_curve(γ, p) cuts a 2π-periodic parametrization $\gamma(\theta)$ into panels. Each panel is the polynomial of degree $p - 1$ interpolating $\gamma$ at $p$ Gauss–Legendre nodes of its interval in $\theta$, stored as Legendre coefficients (CurvePanel); only $\gamma$ itself is needed, not its derivatives. A panel is bisected in $\theta$ until

  • it is resolved: the norm of its last two Legendre coefficients is below tol × its length (with a floor of 1000 ε × the curve's size, for rounding);
  • it is no longer than maxlen, than lencap(x) at its midpoint if that function is given, and than lfs_factor × the local feature size;
  • neighboring panels differ in length by at most a factor 2 (balance).

The local feature size (local_feature_size) combines curvature and thickness,

\[\mathrm{lfs}(x) = \min\Big(\frac{1}{|\kappa(x)|},\ \tfrac12 \min\{|x - y| : y \in \Gamma,\ n(x)\cdot n(y) < 0\}\Big),\]

the second term half the distance to the nearest part of $\Gamma$ facing away from $x$: across a neck of half-width $\delta$ it is about $\delta$, and it also sees two parts of $\Gamma$ approaching from outside. discretize_curve sees one curve at a time; discretize_curves discretizes the curves of a domain together and also limits each panel to gap_factor × its distance to the other curves (a hole near the rim, a narrow ring).

The boundary discretization (BoundaryDisc) holds the Gauss–Legendre nodes of every panel, their arclength weights, tangents, normals and curvatures.

The area mesh

Every area panel is a ruled quadrilateral over $(s, t) \in [-1, 1]^2$,

\[x(s, t) = \tfrac12(1 - t)\,B(s) + \tfrac12(1 + t)\,T(s),\]

with bottom and top curves $B$, $T$ stored as Legendre coefficients, oriented so that $J = \det[\partial_s x\ \partial_t x] > 0$, and a $p \times p$ tensor grid of nodes ($s$ fastest). The quadrature weight of a node is $w = w_s w_t J$. build_mesh(panels, p) builds the mesh in three parts.

The edge layer. One edge panel sits on each boundary panel: its bottom $B$ is the boundary panel's curve, so $t = -1$ lies exactly on $\Gamma$, and its top is the straight segment between inner vertices $w_k = v_k - h_k n_k$ ($v_k$ the start of boundary panel $k$). The strip width is

\[h_k = \min\big(\text{strip\_factor} \cdot \min(L_{k-1}, L_k),\ \text{thick\_factor} \cdot \mathrm{lfs}(v_k)\big),\]

at least 1.5 times the inward bulge of the adjacent panels beyond their chords, and is then shrunk locally until every edge panel has $\min J > \text{jtol} \cdot \max J$, no top segment collapses, and the inner polygon $W = (w_1, \dots, w_N)$ is simple, does not cross $\Gamma$ and keeps a clearance from its other parts. These conditions guarantee that the edge panels tile the strip between $\Gamma$ and $W$; when the repair fails, the error asks for a finer boundary.

The weight at the edge. The solution of a first-kind integral equation on an open surface (the charge on a conducting disc, the traction on a rigid plate) behaves like $d^{-1/2}$ at the edge, $d$ the distance to $\Gamma$. On the edge panels a density is written

\[\mu = \tilde\mu\,(1 + t)^{-1/2},\]

with $\tilde\mu$ smooth: the nodes store $\tilde\mu$, the $t$-rule is the Gauss–Jacobi rule of weight $(1 + t)^{-1/2}$ (from the $2p$-point Gauss–Legendre rule by $t = 2\sigma^2 - 1$), and its weight is part of $w$. These panels are weighted; ad.wfac is $(1 + t)^{-1/2}$ on them and 1 elsewhere.

Graded layers. With edge_layers = L > 1 (default 3) each edge panel is split in $t$ into $L$ layers, with interfaces at $(1 + t)/2 = \sigma^{L-1}, \dots, \sigma$ ($\sigma$ = edge_grading, default 0.2), geometrically graded toward $\Gamma$. Only the layer on $\Gamma$ is weighted. The layers resolve the $d^{1/2}\log d$ terms of a density that is bounded at the edge; a $d^{-1/2}$ density is better served by one layer (Limits). All the layers over boundary panel $k$ form its edge column (bpanel = k).

The interior. The polygon $W$ is meshed by Gmsh (Delaunay, with the target edge length $f(x) = \min(\text{interior\_size}, \min_k(\text{size\_factor}\,\ell_k + \text{grading}\,|x - w_k|))$, $\ell_k$ the length of the edges of $W$ at $w_k$) and recombined into quadrilaterals (Blossom matching); leftover triangles and non-convex quadrilaterals are split into three quadrilaterals each. With coarse_interior = true every other vertex of $W$ is moved onto the segment of its neighbors, so the interior has half as many boundary edges and coarser panels next to the strip (non-conforming there, still an exact tiling).

A domain with holes is one drop with several curves; several drops are meshed one by one and concatenated (drop $i$ owns drop_panels(ad, i); its curves are drop_curves(bd, i), the outer first). mesh_quality reports the Jacobian ratio and aspect ratio of every panel.

Smooth densities

A density that is smooth up to $\Gamma$ (a source term, a concentration) does not have the $d^{-1/2}$ factor, and integrating it as $\mu\,(1 + t)^{1/2}$ with the weighted rule converges only algebraically. With density = :smooth an evaluator integrates it on an unweighted copy of the mesh instead (Gauss–Legendre in $t$ on every panel; on the weighted panels the density is interpolated from their Gauss–Jacobi nodes). interpolate_nodal evaluates the panel interpolant of nodal values at any points.
