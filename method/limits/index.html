Limits

Flat surfaces, targets in the plane

$\Omega$ lies in the plane $z = 0$ and every target is in that plane: on the mesh, on $\Gamma$, or at points $(x, y, 0)$. There is no rule for targets off the plane (a field above the surface), and no curved surfaces.

Smooth edges

The weight $(1 + t)^{-1/2}$ of the edge layer is the singularity of a first-kind density at a smooth edge. $\Gamma$ should be smooth: at a corner the density's singularity has another exponent, which the weight does not represent, and discretize_curve refines toward the corner (up to maxpanels) without resolving it.

Graded layers and edge-singular densities

The graded layers (edge_layers > 1) resolve the $d^{1/2}\log d$ terms of a density that is bounded at the edge. A density with the $d^{-1/2}$ singularity of a first-kind equation is weighted only on the first, thin layer, and continues into the unweighted layers above it, where the plain rule does not represent it. For such densities use one layer: the charged disc at p = 10 is within 1.9e-10 near the edge ($d < 0.05$) with edge_layers = 1, and 2.8e-5 with the default 3 (its total charge within 4.2e-11 and 3.4e-9).

Densities: stored or smooth

A density with the $d^{-1/2}$ edge singularity goes in the stored form (density = :stored), a density smooth up to $\Gamma$ in the smooth form (density = :smooth); either one in the other form converges only algebraically (Getting started).

Close approach

Where two parts of $\Gamma$ come within a gap $g$ (a thin neck, a hole near the rim, two drops), the panels facing the gap must be shorter than about $g$, and the strip of edge panels must fit in it. discretize_curve limits the panels by the local feature size of one curve; between curves, use discretize_curves, since build_mesh meshes several drops independently. When the strip cannot be repaired, build_mesh stops with an error that asks for a finer boundary.

Kernels

  • A kernel that is not scalar declares its shape (kernel_shape); a closure can only be scalar. The evaluator probes the kernel and refuses a 2×2 value it would truncate.
  • Line sources with targets on $\Gamma$ need a kernel bounded at $r = 0$ or one whose split $A\log\rho + S$ is known (LaplaceKernel2D, MHKernel, the 2D Stokeslet); any other is refused.
  • Stokeslet3D and G3 are the half-space Stokeslet (a fluid of viscosity $\mu_{3D}$ below a free surface): for an unbounded fluid of viscosity $\mu_f$, pass $\mu_{3D} = 2\mu_f$.
  • Kernels other than those of the Fast sums table are summed directly, $O(NM)$.

Kernels from symbols

  • The base is given, not extracted from the symbol, and must match it at both ends of $k$.
  • A remainder that decays algebraically needs a large $k_{\max}$: a screened kernel $e^{-\kappa\rho}/4\pi\rho$ needs $k_{\max} \sim 1/(\text{tol}\,\kappa)$, so it warns at the default tolerance. As a remainder of an Ewald sum it is refused (the long-range multiplier would sample its cone at $\rho = 0$); a SymbolKernel used as the kernel itself is summed by Ewald, its remainder split by weight (Fast sums).
  • The table of a SymbolKernel used as the kernel itself under Ewald must reach the long-range window (the point set's extent plus about $11\sigma$): build it with a larger $\rho_c$ or a far field otherwise.
  • regularize (a remainder not integrable at $k = 0$) applies to the scalar :gradient output only.
  • A remainder without a far field is summed locally, over the pairs closer than $\rho_c$, which is all-pairs ($O(N^2)$) when $\rho_c$ exceeds the film. With a far field, BiharmonicGradient2D sums it by Ewald; for LaplaceKernel3D, use the SymbolKernel itself as the kernel.

Fast sums

  • FilmEwald skips only the area self pairs: its area nodes must be distinct and no boundary node may sit on an area node (true of build_mesh, whose area nodes are interior Gauss points); it throws otherwise.
  • An Evaluator of Stokeslet3D (or of a tensor SymbolKernel under Ewald) takes one target set, and a remainder with Ewald (the default) or direct sums, not with FMM3D.
  • MHNormalKernel with several target sets: the direct sum indexes its normals per set, the fast sums over the sets concatenated; use one target set.
  • The accuracy of an Ewald plan or of a remainder's local sum is fixed when it is built; only the FMMs take eps per call.
  • evaluator_matrix is dense, $O(N^2)$ memory: at p = 10 the unit disc has 8700 nodes, a 600 MB matrix for a scalar kernel. Use it for small problems and checks; solve with the operator.
