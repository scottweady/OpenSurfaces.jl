Fast sums

The plain rule is a sum over all (target, source) pairs, $u_i = \sum_j K(x_i - y_j)\,f_j$ with the weights in $f$. Every fast sum here skips coincident pairs (a target exactly on a source), as the local corrections expect. An Evaluator picks the sum by the kernel's type:

KernelSum
Stokeslet3DEwald + NUFFT (EwaldStokes3D; default), or FMM3D (fast = :fmm)
LaplaceKernel3DEwald + NUFFT (EwaldLaplace3D; default), or FMM3D (fast = :fmm)
LaplaceKernel2D, LaplaceGradient2D, BiharmonicGradient2DFMM2D (Laplace; default), or Ewald + NUFFT (RadialEwald2D, fast = :ewald)
MHKernel, MHNormalKernelFMM2D (Helmholtz at the imaginary wavenumber $ik$; default), or Ewald + NUFFT (RadialEwald2D, fast = :ewald)
StokesKernel(μ_2D, μ_3D) (G2 + G3)Ewald + NUFFT (EwaldStokes3D with μ_2D; default)
SymbolKernel of base Stokeslet3D or LaplaceKernel3DEwald + NUFFT of the whole kernel (default)
BiharmonicGradient2D + a remainder with a far fieldEwald + NUFFT of the whole kernel (RadialEwald2D, out = :bgrad; default)
LaplaceKernel3D or BiharmonicGradient2D + a remainder without onelocal sum over the pairs closer than $\rho_c$
any otherdirect

With few targets the default is the direct sum instead, for every kernel above summed by Ewald by default: an Ewald sum costs about the same whatever the number of targets (its grid grows with the sources), while the direct sum grows with sources × targets, so below a number of targets the direct sum wins. That number grows with the accuracy; fast = :auto takes the direct sum below

\[M^*(\text{eps}) = 2000\,\bigl(\log_{10}(1/\text{eps})/6\bigr)^{3.5}\]

targets in all (about 2000 at eps 1e-6, 5500 at 1e-8, 12000 at 1e-10), the crossovers measured on 13.9k sources and 16 threads (1000–2000 targets at 1e-6, 8000–20000 at 1e-10 across these kernels). A boundary's nodes as targets are then summed directly. fast = :ewald (or a plan) keeps Ewald.

The defaults follow timings on 16 threads (studies/laplace_ewald). For the 3D Laplace kernel an Ewald apply is 4–15 times faster than FMM3D from 8.8k to 410k nodes (0.058 s against 0.6 s at 110k nodes, eps 1e-6), and its setup costs about one FMM3D apply. For the 2D kernels FMM2D is as fast or faster at the same error (its error runs about 1e-4 × eps), so it stays the default there.

eps sets the accuracy of the sum (default tol). The FMMs take it per call, E(f; eps); an Ewald plan and the local sum of a remainder fix it when they are built.

FMM

stokes2d_apply and stokes3d_plane_apply wrap FMM2D's and FMM3D's Stokes FMMs in the package's units, for points in the plane. With $G_2 = (-\log\rho\,I + rr^T/\rho^2)/(4\pi\mu_{2D})$ the 2D velocity is the library's divided by $2\pi\mu_{2D}$, and the stress (and so the traction, traction_normals) does not depend on $\mu_{2D}$; with $G_3 = (I/\rho + rr^T/\rho^3)/(4\pi\mu_{3D})$, the in-plane block of the half-space Stokeslet, the 3D velocity is twice the library's divided by $\mu_{3D}$, and its out-of-plane component vanishes. The tests re-check every normalization against direct sums (stokes2d_direct, stokes3d_plane_direct).

FMM3D drops every coincident pair. FMM2D drops the pair $i = j$ but, for any other coincident pair (an explicit target on a source, a duplicated source), adds a spurious $f/(8\pi\mu_{2D})$; stokes2d_apply finds such pairs by hashing and subtracts it.

Ewald splitting for the 3D Stokeslet

For points in a plane the 3D Stokeslet sums faster by Ewald splitting with non-uniform FFTs (FINUFFT) than by FMM3D. With $\phi = 1/\rho$ and $K[\phi](r) = \phi I - (\phi'(\rho)/\rho)\,rr^T$ (so that $K[1/\rho] = 4\pi\mu G_3$), the kernel splits as

\[\frac{1}{\rho} = \underbrace{\frac{\operatorname{erf}(\rho/\sigma)}{\rho}}_{\phi_L,\ \text{smooth}} + \underbrace{\frac{\operatorname{erfc}(\rho/\sigma)}{\rho}}_{\phi_S,\ \text{short range}}, \qquad K = K[\phi_L] + K[\phi_S].\]

  • Long range. $K[\phi_L]$ is smooth. Multiplied by a window equal to 1 over every separation in the point set's bounding box, it has compact support, so its free-space convolution equals a periodic one on twice the support. Its Fourier multiplier is computed once by an FFT of samples; each sum is a type-1 NUFFT (sources to modes), the 2×2 multiplier, and a type-2 NUFFT (modes to targets).
  • Short range. $K[\phi_S]$ decays like $\operatorname{erfc}(\rho/\sigma)$; the pairs closer than $r_c = c\sigma$, $\operatorname{erfc}(c) \approx$ tol, are summed through a sparse matrix built once.
  • Coincident pairs. The short range skips them; the long range's value there, $K[\phi_L](0)f = 2f/(\sigma\sqrt\pi)$, is subtracted, for $i = j$ and for any target exactly on a source.

$\sigma$ is chosen for about nneigh (150) short-range neighbors per target. A smooth remainder (a SymbolKernel, below) is added to the long-range multiplier.

With a finite μ_2D, EwaldStokes3D sums $G_2 + G_3$ (StokesKernel) on one grid, the 2D Stokeslet split as in FilmEwald below; its long-range value at coincident pairs adds $(1 + \gamma_E/2 - \log\sigma)/(4\pi\mu_{2D})$. With few targets (a boundary's nodes) the direct sum is faster, and fast = :auto takes it (above).

EwaldLaplace3D is the same split for the 3D Laplace kernel $1/(4\pi\rho)$: a scalar multiplier, one NUFFT pair per sum, and the self value $\phi_L(0)/4\pi$ subtracted at coincident pairs.

A whole kernel from its symbol

A SymbolKernel used as the kernel itself, of base Stokeslet3D or LaplaceKernel3D, is summed whole by the base's Ewald split plus its remainder $R$, of any decay. $R$ may have a cone or a logarithm at $\rho = 0$ (an algebraically decaying remainder), which a long-range multiplier cannot sample, so it is split by a weight,

\[R = \underbrace{w R}_{\text{long range}} + \underbrace{(1 - w) R}_{\text{short range}},\qquad w(\rho) = P(m, U) = 1 - e^{-U}\sum_{j<m} \frac{U^j}{j!},\quad U = \rho^2/\sigma^2,\ m = 4.\]

$w$ is a sum of Gaussians of width $\sigma$ times polynomials, so the grid of the base's split resolves it, and $w \sim U^m/m!$ at $\rho = 0$ cancels a cone of $R$ to order $2m$. Its leftover Fourier tail is why an algebraic remainder takes a grid twice as fine. $(1 - w) R$ decays like $e^{-U}U^{m-1}$; the short range runs out to where it is below tol. Coincident pairs are skipped ($w R$ vanishes there), as in the direct sum of the whole kernel, whose near corrections hold its singular part. The remainder's table (or its far field) must reach the long-range window, the point set's extent plus about $11\sigma$; the plan refuses it otherwise.

Ewald splitting for the 2D kernels

RadialEwald2D sums $\log\rho/2\pi$ and $K_0(k\rho)/2\pi$ (values, several densities at once, or gradients). Both are heat-kernel integrals, $\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_0^\infty e^{-sk^2 - \rho^2/4s}\,ds/s$ up to sign and a constant, split at $s = \sigma^2/4$: the long range has the Fourier transform $e^{-\sigma^2(k^2 + |\xi|^2)/4}/(k^2 + |\xi|^2)$ and the short range decays like $E_1(\rho^2/\sigma^2)$. For $\log\rho$ both parts are closed forms (the short range $-E_1(U)/4\pi$); for $K_0$ the long range is tabulated once per plan on $\rho \le \sqrt{40}\,\sigma$ and equals $K_0$ beyond. A gradient multiplies the multiplier by $i\xi$. BiharmonicGradient2D is three log sums and moments, as on its FMM path.

With a remainder (a finite depth: symbol $\Phi(kH)/k^4$ over the deep $1/k^4$), out = :bgrad sums the whole kernel $r f(\rho)$, $f = (2\log\rho + c_0)/8\pi + q(\rho)$, with $q$ the remainder's regularized :gradient and $c_0$ the deep kernel's, which together make $f$ decay. The same split of $\log\rho$ gives the short range $-r E_1(U)/8\pi$ and the long range $r[(2\log_L\rho + c_0)/8\pi + q]$, sampled as a two-component kernel, so the deep part and $q$ cancel when the multiplier is built rather than in each apply (the moments of the deep path would lose about $L\log L/H$ in relative accuracy). An apply is one type-1 NUFFT, a 2 × 1 multiplier and a type-2 NUFFT with two outputs. Both parts vanish at $r = 0$. The grid resolves $q$ ($\sigma \le cH/10$), and the remainder's table or its far field must reach the long-range window. The plan checks that $f$ decays at the table's end, which catches a $c_0$ (a $\kappa$) that is not the remainder's. On a film 9 across at $H = 2$, eps 1e-7, 72k nodes, an apply takes 0.2 s against 32 s for the local sum, which is all-pairs there ($\rho_c = 20.5$).

FilmEwald evaluates, on one grid, all the far-field sums of a coupled 2D + 3D Stokes operator on a surface and its edge: the area-to-area velocity $\sum_j [G_2 + G_3](x_i - y_j)f_j$, the edge-to-area velocity $\sum_j G_2(x_i - z_j)g_j$, and the area-to-edge traction of the 2D part. The 2D Stokeslet is split by Hasimoto's screening of the biharmonic ($U = \rho^2/\sigma^2$), $-\log\rho\,I + rr^T/\rho^2 = S_L + S_S$, $S_S = (\tfrac12 E_1(U) - e^{-U})I + e^{-U}rr^T/\rho^2$.

Smooth convolutions

SmoothConvolution sums a kernel that is smooth on a given length scale (the remainder of a kernel from its symbol) over all pairs, including coincident ones, by the long-range half of the Ewald method alone.

Threads

The FMMs, the NUFFTs and the corrections run on Julia's threads. FINUFFT and FFTW.jl share a libfftw3 whose threads callback (installed by FFTW.jl) FINUFFT's threaded FFTs cannot run under; the package removes the callback when it loads with more than one thread, which leaves FFTW its own threads (the setting is process-wide).
