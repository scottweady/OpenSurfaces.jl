Fast sums

The plain rule is a sum over all (target, source) pairs, $u_i = \sum_j K(x_i - y_j)\,f_j$ with the weights in $f$. Every fast sum here skips coincident pairs (a target exactly on a source), as the local corrections expect. An Evaluator picks the sum by the kernel's type:

KernelSum
Stokeslet3DEwald + NUFFT (EwaldStokes3D; default), or FMM3D (fast = :fmm)
LaplaceKernel3DFMM3D (Laplace)
LaplaceKernel2D, LaplaceGradient2D, BiharmonicGradient2DFMM2D (Laplace)
MHKernel, MHNormalKernelFMM2D (Helmholtz at the imaginary wavenumber $ik$)
any otherdirect

eps sets the accuracy of the sum (default tol). The FMMs take it per call, E(f; eps); an Ewald plan and the local sum of a remainder fix it when they are built.

FMM

stokes2d_apply and stokes3d_plane_apply wrap FMM2D's and FMM3D's Stokes FMMs in the package's units, for points in the plane. With $G_2 = (-\log\rho\,I + rr^T/\rho^2)/(4\pi\mu_{2D})$ the 2D velocity is the library's divided by $2\pi\mu_{2D}$, and the stress (and so the traction, traction_normals) does not depend on $\mu_{2D}$; with $G_3 = (I/\rho + rr^T/\rho^3)/(4\pi\mu_{3D})$, the in-plane block of the half-space Stokeslet, the 3D velocity is twice the library's divided by $\mu_{3D}$, and its out-of-plane component vanishes. The tests re-check every normalization against direct sums (stokes2d_direct, stokes3d_plane_direct).

FMM3D drops every coincident pair. FMM2D drops the pair $i = j$ but, for any other coincident pair (an explicit target on a source, a duplicated source), adds a spurious $f/(8\pi\mu_{2D})$; stokes2d_apply finds such pairs by hashing and subtracts it.

Ewald splitting for the 3D Stokeslet

For points in a plane the 3D Stokeslet sums faster by Ewald splitting with non-uniform FFTs (FINUFFT) than by FMM3D. With $\phi = 1/\rho$ and $K[\phi](r) = \phi I - (\phi'(\rho)/\rho)\,rr^T$ (so that $K[1/\rho] = 4\pi\mu G_3$), the kernel splits as

\[\frac{1}{\rho} = \underbrace{\frac{\operatorname{erf}(\rho/\sigma)}{\rho}}_{\phi_L,\ \text{smooth}} + \underbrace{\frac{\operatorname{erfc}(\rho/\sigma)}{\rho}}_{\phi_S,\ \text{short range}}, \qquad K = K[\phi_L] + K[\phi_S].\]

  • Long range. $K[\phi_L]$ is smooth. Multiplied by a window equal to 1 over every separation in the point set's bounding box, it has compact support, so its free-space convolution equals a periodic one on twice the support. Its Fourier multiplier is computed once by an FFT of samples; each sum is a type-1 NUFFT (sources to modes), the 2×2 multiplier, and a type-2 NUFFT (modes to targets).
  • Short range. $K[\phi_S]$ decays like $\operatorname{erfc}(\rho/\sigma)$; the pairs closer than $r_c = c\sigma$, $\operatorname{erfc}(c) \approx$ tol, are summed through a sparse matrix built once.
  • Coincident pairs. The short range skips them; the long range's value there, $K[\phi_L](0)f = 2f/(\sigma\sqrt\pi)$, is subtracted, for $i = j$ and for any target exactly on a source.

$\sigma$ is chosen for about nneigh (150) short-range neighbors per target. A smooth remainder (a SymbolKernel, below) is added to the long-range multiplier.

FilmEwald evaluates, on one grid, all the far-field sums of a coupled 2D + 3D Stokes operator on a surface and its edge: the area-to-area velocity $\sum_j [G_2 + G_3](x_i - y_j)f_j$, the edge-to-area velocity $\sum_j G_2(x_i - z_j)g_j$, and the area-to-edge traction of the 2D part. The 2D Stokeslet is split by Hasimoto's screening of the biharmonic ($U = \rho^2/\sigma^2$), $-\log\rho\,I + rr^T/\rho^2 = S_L + S_S$, $S_S = (\tfrac12 E_1(U) - e^{-U})I + e^{-U}rr^T/\rho^2$.

Smooth convolutions

SmoothConvolution sums a kernel that is smooth on a given length scale (the remainder of a kernel from its symbol) over all pairs, including coincident ones, by the long-range half of the Ewald method alone.

Threads

The FMMs, the NUFFTs and the corrections run on Julia's threads. FINUFFT and FFTW.jl share a libfftw3 whose threads callback (installed by FFTW.jl) FINUFFT's threaded FFTs cannot run under; the package removes the callback when it loads with more than one thread, which leaves FFTW its own threads (the setting is process-wide).
